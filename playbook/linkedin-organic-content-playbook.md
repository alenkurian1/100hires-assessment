# LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy Playbook

## Introduction

This playbook is based on research conducted on ten leading B2B SaaS marketing experts who regularly share insights on LinkedIn growth, personal branding, demand generation, content marketing, and audience building.

The objective is to combine their best practices into a practical Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for building a strong LinkedIn presence that attracts qualified audiences, builds credibility, and supports long-term business growth.

---

# Goal

Build a repeatable LinkedIn organic content system that:

- Builds authority in a chosen niche
- Attracts the right audience
- Generates inbound leads
- Creates trust through valuable content
- Supports long-term B2B SaaS growth

---

# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## Step 1 – Define Your Niche

Choose one clear audience and one problem you want to solve.

**Source:**
- Justin Welsh
- Amanda Natividad

---

## Step 2 – Optimize Your LinkedIn Profile

Create a profile that clearly explains:

- Who you help
- What results you create
- Why people should follow you

**Source:**
- Austin Belcak
- Jasmin Alic

---

## Step 3 – Build Content Pillars

Create 3–5 recurring topics.

Example:

- Industry insights
- Personal experiences
- Educational posts
- Case studies
- Customer success stories

**Source:**
- Justin Welsh
- Dave Gerhardt

---

## Step 4 – Publish Consistently

Publish 3–5 quality posts every week instead of posting randomly.

Consistency builds trust.

**Source:**
- Justin Welsh
- Ross Simmonds

---

## Step 5 – Focus on Education Before Promotion

Teach first.

Avoid constant selling.

Provide useful frameworks, examples, and lessons.

**Source:**
- Chris Walker
- Amanda Natividad

---

## Step 6 – Engage With Your Audience

Reply to comments.

Comment on industry leaders' posts.

Start meaningful conversations.

**Source:**
- Mandy McEwen
- Lara Acosta

---

## Step 7 – Repurpose Content

Turn one good idea into multiple formats.

Example:

- LinkedIn post
- Carousel
- Newsletter
- Video
- Podcast clip

**Source:**
- Ross Simmonds

---

## Step 8 – Measure Performance

Track:

- Impressions
- Comments
- Saves
- Shares
- Profile visits
- Leads generated

Adjust your strategy monthly.

**Source:**
- Dave Gerhardt

# Where Experts Disagree

## 1. Posting Frequency

**Justin Welsh**
- Recommends posting every weekday to build consistency and audience trust.

**Chris Walker**
- Focuses more on publishing high-quality educational content than posting daily.

**My Decision**
I recommend posting 3–5 high-quality posts per week. Maintaining quality is more sustainable than posting every day.

---

## 2. Personal Stories vs Educational Content

**Lara Acosta**
- Encourages sharing personal experiences to build authenticity and stronger audience connections.

**Amanda Natividad**
- Prioritizes educational and value-driven content over personal storytelling.

**My Decision**
A balanced approach works best. Educational content should be the foundation, supported by occasional personal stories to build trust.

---

## 3. Measuring Success

**Dave Gerhardt**
- Focuses on engagement metrics such as comments, shares, and audience interaction.

**Chris Walker**
- Believes business outcomes such as qualified leads and revenue are more important than vanity metrics.

**My Decision**
Track both engagement and business outcomes. Engagement indicates audience interest, while leads demonstrate business impact.

---

# What I Rejected and Why

## Idea 1

**Rejected:** Posting multiple times every day.

**Reason:**
This can reduce content quality and may overwhelm followers. Consistency with valuable content is more effective.

---

## Idea 2

**Rejected:** Using engagement bait (e.g., "Comment YES", "Like if you agree").

**Reason:**
Although it may increase short-term engagement, it often lowers content quality and weakens audience trust.

---

# My Original Idea

## Monthly LinkedIn Content Audit

At the end of every month, review the top-performing posts and identify:

- Topics that generated the most engagement
- Formats that performed best
- Questions frequently asked by the audience

Use these insights to plan the next month's content calendar.

I believe this approach improves content quality through continuous learning and data-driven decision making.

---

# Weaknesses of This Playbook

- The recommendations are based primarily on successful B2B SaaS creators and may not apply to every industry.
- LinkedIn's algorithm changes over time, requiring ongoing adjustments.
- Some recommendations are based on personal experience shared by experts rather than controlled experiments.
- Results depend heavily on content quality and audience relevance.
